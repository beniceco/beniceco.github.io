Charging terms in plain words

Charger boxes are full of abbreviations. Here is what the common ones mean, in one or two sentences each.

USB-C
The shape of the connector, nothing more. A USB-C port can be slow or fast; the markings below tell you which.
PD (USB Power Delivery)
The standard way for a charger and a device to agree on a voltage higher than 5 V, so more power flows through the same cable. Most phones, tablets and laptops sold today support it.
PPS (Programmable Power Supply)
An option inside PD that lets the voltage be adjusted in small steps during charging. Some phones, notably many Samsung models, need it for their fastest mode.
QC (Quick Charge)
An older fast-charging system from Qualcomm, common on USB-A chargers. Newer versions work alongside PD.
GaN
Gallium nitride, a material used for the transistors inside a charger. It wastes less energy as heat, so a GaN charger can be much smaller for the same power. See the gallium nitride article for the physics.
e-marker
A small chip inside a USB-C cable that tells the charger the cable can carry 5 A. Without it the charger limits itself to 3 A, which is 60 W at 20 V.
EPR (Extended Power Range)
The part of PD that allows up to 48 V and 240 W, for large laptops. It needs a cable rated for 240 W.
Wh and mAh
Watt-hours measure energy; milliamp-hours measure charge. Airlines and engineers use Wh because it does not depend on voltage.
Qi and Qi2
The wireless charging standards. Qi2 adds magnets so the phone lines up with the coil every time, similar to Apple's MagSafe. The Qi standard article has the details.
