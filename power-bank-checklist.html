A short checklist before buying a power bank

Everything you need to judge a power bank is printed on it or on the box. These seven points take a minute to check and catch most bad buys.

  1. Capacity in watt-hours, not only mAh. A proper label shows both. If the Wh figure is missing, multiply mAh by 3.7 and divide by 1000 to estimate it.
  2. Rated output of each port. Look for the watts on the USB-C port. 20 W is enough for phones; laptops need 45 W or more.
  3. Fast-charging standard. "PD" on a USB-C port means it will fast-charge most current phones and tablets.
  4. Recharge input. A bank that only accepts 10 W input can take many hours to refill. 18 W or more is comfortable.
  5. Safety markings. Recognised certification marks and a named manufacturer with a traceable address are basic signs of a genuine product.
  6. Weight that matches the capacity. Lithium cells are heavy. A 20,000 mAh bank that feels very light is a warning sign of fewer cells than claimed.
  7. Airline limit if you travel. Up to 100 Wh can go in cabin baggage on most airlines; power banks are never allowed in checked baggage. The FAA PackSafe page summarises the rules.
